## 1. TITAN EDGE: Build Prestige

### The brief
Titan is strongly associated with watches in India. The challenge was to make Titan Edge read as a **sophisticated, premium design statement**, competing for perception with Rado, Tissot, Longines and Seiko. Creative idea: *"For those who have arrived."*

### Media objective (measurable)
Reach **65% of affluent 28–45 urban professionals, entrepreneurs and senior executives** across 6 cities at an **average frequency of 6+** over 12 weeks, **only in premium environments**, to lift premium brand association by 10 points.

### Media strategy: every medium has a job

| Medium | Job | % | ₹ | Why this medium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube / CTV / OTT | Long-form brand film | 20% | ₹1.60 cr | Room for the full brand film; premium OTT and CTV reach the TG at home, ad-light and targeted |
| OOH | Premium physical presence | 17% | ₹1.36 cr | Airports, business districts and luxury malls: places the TG passes daily, and where luxury brands are seen |
| Instagram / Meta | Retargeting + design-led storytelling | 14% | ₹1.12 cr | Showcases design detail through premium photography; retargets film viewers |
| TV | Stature and credibility | 10% | ₹80 L | Selective premium English news, business and live sports; a TV presence signals a "serious" brand |
| Print | Prestige and credentials | 10% | ₹80 L | ET, Mint, GQ and luxury magazines: the TG trusts them and luxury watches live there |
| Search | Capture demand the campaign creates | 8% | ₹64 L | Prestige media triggers searches; we make sure Titan Edge owns them |
| Influencers / Creators | Third-party endorsement | 8% | ₹64 L | Design, style and business creators lend credibility a brand can't claim for itself |
| Retail / E-commerce | Convert interest at premium point of sale | 6% | ₹48 L | Titan boutiques and Tata CLiQ: turning interest into store visits and enquiries |
| Cinema | Captive, larger-than-life viewing | 5% | ₹40 L | Premium multiplex screens make the brand film feel aspirational |
| Radio | Reach executives in their cars | 2% | ₹16 L | Light commute-time reminder in metros; kept small on purpose |

### Reach, frequency and media weight
- **Target reach:** 65% of TG
- **Frequency:** 6+ average across 12 weeks
- **Frequency cap:** 3 exposures per person per week on digital
- **Why these levels?** Prestige is built through repetition in the right places. A narrow, affluent audience needs to see the brand often enough that it becomes familiar and "established," but a cap keeps it from feeling pushy. Loud and constant would cheapen it.
- **Key assumption:** this TG is light on mass TV and heavy on OTT, airports, business media and Instagram, so premium digital and OOH do most of the work.

### How we'll know it worked

| Objective | KPI | Target | How it's measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build premium perception | Premium association score | +10 points vs pre-campaign | Pre and post brand-lift survey among the TG |
| Reach the right audience | Reach % and average frequency | 65% at 6+ | Platform reports, BARC for TV, OOH audience estimates |
| Create interest | Branded search volume | +30% vs pre-campaign baseline | Google Ads search data and Google Trends |
| Convert at retail | Store visits and high-value enquiries | +15% visits, +20% enquiries | Boutique footfall counters, enquiry logs, Tata CLiQ traffic |

### Why this plan
- **Media mix:** chosen for the quality of the environment, not the size of the audience.
- **Budget split:** the biggest shares go to YouTube/OTT and OOH, because prestige needs to be *seen and felt*.
- **Geographies:** Mumbai and Delhi NCR get over half the budget because that's where the deepest HNI and executive networks are.
- **Reach / frequency:** a small audience reached often builds premium association.
- **Single biggest strategic choice:** **environment over volume.** 37% of the budget goes to YouTube/OTT and premium OOH, while mass TV is kept to just 10%. Being seen in the right places matters more than being seen everywhere.

### Planner's defence
- **Why this medium?** Every medium either tells the story (film), places the brand in premium spaces (OOH, print, cinema), or converts interest (search, retail).
- **Why this amount?** Spend follows the job: storytelling and premium presence first, conversion second, light reminders last.
- **Why this city?** Weighted by where the affluent TG actually lives and works.
- **Why this frequency?** 6+ builds familiarity; a weekly cap stops it from feeling desperate.
- **Why this schedule?** Bursts match the moments when people actually buy premium watches: launches, festive gifting and weddings.

## 2. DANTKANTI PRO+: Get People to Try It

### The brief
A hypothetical premium-mass toothpaste extension from Patanjali, launching into a market crowded with Colgate, Sensodyne, Pepsodent and Dabur. People already know DantKanti; the job was to get them to **actually put the new product in their basket**. Creative idea: *"A new reason to change your toothpaste."*

### Media objective (measurable)
Reach **60% of urban 18–35 SEC A/B consumers** across 7 cities at **3+ frequency** within the 8-week launch, and turn that awareness into **trial and first purchase**, with most buyers being **new to DantKanti**.

### Media strategy: every medium has a job

| Medium | Job | % | ₹ | Why this medium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram / Meta | Discovery and awareness | 15% | ₹1.20 cr | Where 18–35s discover new products; the 15–20s demo video works natively here |
| Search | Information and intent | 15% | ₹1.20 cr | Captures people who saw the ad and want to know more, then routes them to buy |
| TV | Discovery and trial | 10% | ₹80 L | Fastest way to build mass reach for a household product |
| YouTube / CTV / OTT | Discovery and trial | 10% | ₹80 L | Carries the product-benefit demo to a young, digital audience |
| OOH | Launch awareness and promo | 10% | ₹80 L | Near modern retail and transit hubs, as a reminder close to the point of purchase |
| Influencers / Creators | Trials and launch buzz | 10% | ₹80 L | Real people trying it makes trial feel low-risk; drives UGC and reviews |
| Retail / E-commerce | Purchase and trial | 10% | ₹80 L | Shelf visibility, quick-commerce and e-com offers turn interest into a first purchase |
| Radio | Awareness | 8% | ₹64 L | Cost-efficient frequency in cities with heavy commutes |
| Cinema | Discovery with a captive audience | 5% | ₹40 L | Young audiences, full attention, big-screen demo |
| Other: product sampling | Put the product in people's hands | 5% | ₹40 L | Free samples at colleges, gyms and housing societies: the most literal answer to the brief |
| Print | Launch information | 2% | ₹16 L | Kept small; only for launch announcements and offers |
| **Total** | | **100%** | **₹8.00 cr** | |

### Geographic allocation

Our logic: **establish** the product in developing markets and **push for growth** in developed ones.

| City | % | ₹ | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyderabad | 17.5% | ₹1.40 cr | Our biggest bet: a large young urban base, strong modern retail and quick-commerce, and room to establish against the category leaders |
| Delhi NCR | 15% | ₹1.20 cr | Largest market; push for growth where DantKanti already has awareness |
| Mumbai | 15% | ₹1.20 cr | Huge young population, but expensive and cluttered, so held at 15% |
| Pune | 15% | ₹1.20 cr | Students and young professionals who are quick to try new products; high e-commerce use |
| Bengaluru | 12.5% | ₹1.00 cr | Digital-first city with strong e-commerce; digital media does most of the work |
| Ahmedabad | 12.5% | ₹1.00 cr | Developing market for the premium-mass segment; establish early |
| Lucknow | 12.5% | ₹1.00 cr | Growing urban young-consumer base; establish early |
| **Total** | **100%** | **₹8.00 cr** | |

### Reach, frequency and media weight
- **Target reach:** 60% of TG
- **Frequency:** 3+
- **Frequency cap:** 4 exposures per person per week on digital
- **Why these levels?** A launch needs breadth: as many new people as possible need to hear about it quickly. 3+ exposures is enough to register a new benefit and become familiar, but not annoying.
- **Key assumption:** toothpaste is a low-involvement, habit-driven purchase, so trial depends on visibility close to the shelf plus a low-risk offer (coupon, trial pack or free sample).

### Campaign schedule: front-loaded launch, then sustained (8 weeks)
A heavy burst in weeks 1–2, then a steady baseline, with retail pushes timed to salary days and e-commerce sale periods.

*Note: our worksheet ticked "flighting," but heavy-burst-then-sustained is closer to **pulsing** (always on, with bursts). Flighting means switching off completely between bursts.*

### How we'll know it worked

| Objective | KPI | Target | How it's measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build awareness | Aided awareness of Pro+ among TG | 40% by week 8 | Brand tracking survey |
| Drive trial | Share of Pro+ buyers who are new to DantKanti | 80% | E-com and retail purchase data, coupon redemption data |
| Create buzz | Growth in reviews, UGC and online mentions | +20% week on week during launch | Social listening, e-com review counts |
| Grow, don't cannibalise | Share of Pro+ buyers switching from core DantKanti | Under 30% | Retail panel / purchase data comparison |

### Why this plan
- **Media mix:** digital-heavy for sustained frequency with a young audience, backed by retail and sampling to close the trial.
- **Budget split:** more intensity in developing markets; find the audience where they already are.
- **Geographies:** establish in developing markets, push for growth in developed ones.
- **Reach / frequency:** enough to be familiar, not enough to be annoying.
- **Single biggest strategic choice:** **betting on Hyderabad.** It gets the largest single share (17.5%), more than Mumbai or Delhi, because it's where a new premium-mass toothpaste has the most room to establish itself.

### Planner's defence
- **Why this medium?** Each medium moves people one step along awareness → search → offer → trial → purchase.
- **Why this amount?** Discovery (social, search, video) gets the most because trial starts with knowing the product exists.
- **Why this city?** Developing markets get more intensity to establish the product; developed markets get enough to grow.
- **Why this frequency?** 3+ is enough to register a new benefit without wear-out.
- **Why this schedule?** A launch has to be loud at the start, and buying happens around salary days and sale periods.
